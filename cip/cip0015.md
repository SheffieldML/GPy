# CIP-0015: INLA-style hyperparameter integration for Laplace GP models

## Summary

Add an optional wrapper that integrates over GP hyperparameters for models using the `Laplace` inference method, following the INLA recipe: a Laplace approximation to p(f | θ, y) at each of a small set of θ values, weighted by an approximation to p(θ | y). The first version is a dense-covariance, GPy-native wrapper. It does not reproduce INLA's sparse-precision scalability.

## Motivation

GPy's Laplace inference fits one Gaussian at the latent mode for fixed θ, and θ is normally set by MAP or type-II maximum likelihood. This understates predictive uncertainty. In the prototype below, the latent predictive variance at test points rose by up to a factor of 1.7 once the lengthscale and variance were integrated out, and the predictive means moved by up to 0.2 on the latent scale. The change matters most away from the data, where lengthscale uncertainty dominates.

GPy already has the pieces: a Laplace marginal likelihood, hyperparameter priors, and (since commit `dae930d4`) a requirement that transform Jacobians enter the target density. What is missing is the layer that explores θ and mixes predictions.

## Detailed Description

Work in the unconstrained optimiser space u (GPy's `optimizer_array`), so positivity constraints become a smooth transform and a Gaussian approximation to p(u | y) is reasonable.

1. **Mode and curvature.** Optimise to the MAP u\*. Estimate the Hessian H of −log p(u | y) by central differences of the model gradient. Set L so that L Lᵀ = H⁻¹.
2. **Design.** Evaluate at points u = u\* + L z. Three designs are supported:
   - *Grid*: greedy outward exploration in z with step 1.0, dropping points more than 3 nats below the maximum. Suitable for up to about 3 hyperparameters.
   - *CCD*: centre, 2d star points at radius 1.1 √d, and 2^d corners (R-INLA's default for more hyperparameters).
   - *Importance*: antithetic multivariate-t draws in z, scaled separately on each side of each axis (skew-aware), weighted by p/q. Fixed budget, scales to larger d.
3. **Weights.** w_k ∝ exp(−`model._objective(u_k)`), normalised. This is log p(y | u) + log p(u), including Jacobian terms for parameters that carry priors.
4. **Prediction.** For each u_k set the model parameters, call the existing `predict` or `predict_noiseless`, and combine with the law of total variance. This gives the mixture mean and variance, not a full mixture density.

### Design findings from the prototype

The prototype is `cip/cip0015/inla_prototype.py`, with a check against dense quadrature in `cip/cip0015/check_inla_prototype.py`. Setup: 25 Bernoulli observations, RBF kernel with LogGaussian priors on both hyperparameters.

| Method | Points | Max abs error, latent mean | Max abs error, latent variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid | 34 | 0.052 | 0.101 |
| CCD | 9 | 0.068 | 0.599 |
| Dense 41×41 quadrature (reference) | 1681 | n/a | n/a |

Observations that shape the design:

- **Priors are load-bearing.** `log_prior` adds the Jacobian only for parameters with a prior. Without priors, the density is flat in u, which is improper and depends on the parameterisation. The wrapper should either require priors on all free parameters or warn loudly.
- **CCD is cheap but coarse.** At d = 2 its variance error was six times the grid's. See the d >= 4 section below: it is also coarse for hyperparameter marginals.
- **Warm starts are disabled.** `Laplace.inference` overwrites its warm start with zeros (a `FIXME` in the source), so each design point runs a full Newton fit. Cost is the number of design points times one Laplace fit. See "Laplace warm start" below for the measurement.
- **Gradients are used for the Hessian.** This relies on the Laplace gradient being accurate. The prototype floored non-positive eigenvalues; the implementation raises instead.
- **Only moments are mixed.** Mixing full predictive densities (as INLA does for marginals) needs a per-point quantile or density interface that GPy does not currently expose uniformly.

### Accuracy at d >= 4 and against HMC (2026-11-27)

`cip/cip0015/check_high_dim.py` uses an ARD RBF with a Gaussian likelihood and fixed noise (cheap evaluations; the wrapper does not depend on the likelihood). The reference is self-normalised importance sampling in standardised z-space with a multivariate-t proposal (20,000 draws, effective sample size 417 at d = 4 and 2138 at d = 5). For d = 4 we cross-checked it against HMC with 5,000 draws on the same target; the two agree (for example lengthscale 2: HMC 7.45 ± 5.76, importance sampling 7.86 ± 6.09). Errors are for 30 test inputs, with the mean error scaled by the mean reference predictive standard deviation.

| d | Method | Points | Max latent-mean error (sd) | Hyperparameter sd, max relative error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | MAP plug-in | 1 | 0.74 | n/a |
| 4 | CCD | 25 | 0.45 | 0.70 |
| 4 | Grid (cutoff 9, step 1.5) | 1333 | 0.085 | 0.21 |
| 5 | MAP plug-in | 1 | 0.49 | n/a |
| 5 | CCD | 43 | 0.32 | 0.69 |
| 5 | Grid (cutoff 9, step 1.5) | 6264 | 0.049 | 0.30 |

Findings:

- **The grid cutoff matters a great deal.** With the prototype default (3 nats) the grid understated the spread of weakly identified lengthscales by about 50% (d = 4 lengthscale 2: 5.7 ± 2.8 against 7.9 ± 6.1). These lengthscales have heavy right tails on the constrained scale. Widening the cutoff to 9 nats with step 1.5 recovers HMC's marginals (7.3 ± 5.2). A cutoff of 6 with step 1.0 was still low (6.8 ± 4.4). We changed the defaults to cutoff 9 and step 1.5.
- **CCD is too coarse for hyperparameter marginals** at these dimensions: its hyperparameter standard deviations are off by about 70%, and its predictive means improve on the MAP only modestly (0.74 to 0.45 sd at d = 4). It is fine as a cheap hedge but not as an accurate integrator.
- **The grid is accurate but grows quickly:** 1333 points at d = 4 and 6264 at d = 5. With the cheap Gaussian likelihood this took 13 s and 37 s; a Laplace fit per point will cost more. Beyond about d = 5 a different design is needed. An importance-sampling design (fixed budget of draws from a heavy-tailed proposal in z-space, weighted by p/q, with the effective sample size reported) is the obvious candidate. The reference code in `check_high_dim.py` already implements it.
- **Variance is well recovered everywhere:** the ratio of the design's predictive variance to the reference stays within 0.85 to 1.15 for CCD and 0.93 to 1.05 for the grid.
- **Bug found by the check:** `hyperparameter_summary` mixed up `param_array` (which includes fixed parameters) with the free parameter names whenever a parameter was fixed. Fixed, with a regression test.

HMC comparison notebook: `cip/cip0015/inla_vs_hmc.ipynb` (25 Bernoulli points, d = 2, same target for both methods). The INLA design needed 155 Laplace fits and 2.9 s; HMC needed 900 kept draws and 55 s. Against HMC, the design's maximum errors were 0.040 (latent mean), 0.041 (latent variance) and 0.010 (P(y = 1)); the MAP plug-in's were 0.251, 1.301 and 0.053. HMC acceptance was 0.99, so its step size was conservative and its own Monte Carlo error is not negligible for the heavy-tailed variance hyperparameter.

### Importance-sampling design (2026-11-27)

Following the d >= 4 results, we added `design="importance"`: antithetic multivariate-t draws in standardised z-space (5 degrees of freedom, scale 1.2, fixed seed), weighted by p/q. It has a fixed budget (`n_samples`, default 500) and exposes `effective_sample_size`; `design="auto"` now uses the grid for up to 3 hyperparameters and importance above that. Same benchmark as above:

| d | Method | Points | Effective sample size | Max latent-mean error (sd) | Hyperparameter sd, max relative error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | CCD | 25 | 10 | 0.45 | 0.70 |
| 4 | Grid | 1333 | 58 | 0.085 | 0.21 |
| 4 | Importance | 250 | 43 | 0.195 | 0.39 |
| 4 | Importance | 1000 | 312 | 0.117 | 0.44 |
| 4 | Importance | 4000 | 801 | 0.033 | 0.38 |
| 5 | CCD | 43 | 13 | 0.32 | 0.69 |
| 5 | Grid | 6264 | 212 | 0.049 | 0.30 |
| 5 | Importance | 250 | 77 | 0.177 | 0.37 |
| 5 | Importance | 1000 | 155 | 0.065 | 0.22 |
| 5 | Importance | 4000 | 692 | 0.065 | 0.22 |

- At d = 5, 1000 importance points match the 6264-point grid to within 0.02 sd on predictive means, with six times fewer Laplace fits. At d = 4 the 1333-point grid is slightly better than 1000 importance draws (0.085 against 0.117), and 4000 draws are better still.
- Even 250 draws beat CCD on predictive means (0.18 to 0.20 sd against 0.32 to 0.45), at ten times the cost.
- Hyperparameter standard deviations are noisy across budgets (0.2 to 0.4 relative error). The reference itself has effective sample size 417 at d = 4, and the heavy-tailed variance hyperparameter is hard to pin down, so differences between budgets of this size should not be over-read.
- **Limitation.** The proposal is symmetric in z, but real posteriors can be strongly skewed. In a concentrated 4-input ARD model with 30 points (d = 5), the log density fell by 25 nats at z = -3 along one axis but by only 1.6 at z = +3, and the largest weights sat near z = +7. The effective sample size was 35 out of 2000 draws (2.3 out of 500). The wrapper warns when the effective sample size drops below 20. The skew-aware proposal below addresses this.

### Skew-aware importance proposal (2026-11-27)

The symmetric proposal wastes draws when the posterior is steep on one side and heavy-tailed on the other. `INLAHyperparameters(..., skew=True)` (now the default for the importance design) scales each standardised axis separately on its positive and negative sides, in the spirit of R-INLA's skewness correction. Each side's scale is `t / sqrt(2 * drop)`, where `drop` is the log-density loss at distance `t = 2` (clipped to [0.25, 4]). Draws are `z = z0 * s`, with `z0` the multivariate-t draw and `s` the scale for each coordinate's sign, so the proposal density stays exact: `log q(z) = log t(z0) - sum(log s)`. The cost is 2d extra density evaluations.

Mean over five design seeds, same benchmark as above:

| d | Points | Skew | Effective sample size | Max latent-mean error (sd) | Hyperparameter sd, max relative error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 250 | no | 66 | 0.239 ± 0.134 | 0.41 ± 0.11 |
| 4 | 250 | yes | 130 | 0.152 ± 0.101 | 0.31 ± 0.11 |
| 4 | 1000 | no | 270 | 0.104 ± 0.049 | 0.46 ± 0.04 |
| 4 | 1000 | yes | 467 | 0.074 ± 0.040 | 0.32 ± 0.07 |
| 5 | 250 | no | 57 | 0.142 ± 0.067 | 0.37 ± 0.15 |
| 5 | 250 | yes | 105 | 0.108 ± 0.057 | 0.22 ± 0.07 |
| 5 | 1000 | no | 132 | 0.056 ± 0.020 | 0.29 ± 0.06 |
| 5 | 1000 | yes | 298 | 0.055 ± 0.021 | 0.30 ± 0.11 |

- The effective sample size roughly doubles in every configuration, and the errors fall in all but one: at d = 5 with 1000 points the two proposals are indistinguishable. Skew helps most where draws are scarce.
- On the concentrated 4-input model that motivated this work, the effective sample size rose from 2 to 60 at 500 draws, and from 35 to 82 at 2000. Fitted scales on one axis were 1.49 (plus) against 0.47 (minus).
- The effective sample size remains a small fraction of the draws on that model, because the heavy right tail is still only approximated by a scaled t. A proposal adapted from the weighted draws (for example a second stage) is a possible further step, and is not planned.
- A single-seed comparison is noisy at this level: the d = 4 reference has effective sample size 417, so differences of 0.03 sd are within its own error.

### Design decisions (confirmed 2026-11-27)

1. **Post-hoc wrapper, not an inference class.** An inference method's `inference()` returns one `Posterior` for fixed θ and is called inside the θ optimisation. A θ-mixture does not fit that interface, and making `GP.predict` mixture-aware would touch every model. The wrapper is additive, works with any GPy model that exposes `_objective` and `predict`, and can later be delegated to from a thin `m.predict` hook. The cost is that users must call the wrapper explicitly, and it relies on private `_objective` methods.
2. **Location:** `GPy/inference/hyperparameters/inla.py`.
3. **Priors are required** on every free hyperparameter; the wrapper raises otherwise.
4. **A Hessian that is not positive definite raises** `LinAlgError` with a message, rather than being repaired silently.
5. **Moments only** in the first version; no mixture densities or quantiles, and no simplified-Laplace or skewness corrections.
6. **Sparse precision structure (GMRF, SPDE)** is out of scope.

### Laplace warm start

We tried removing the `FIXME` line in `Laplace.inference` that zeroes the warm start. On the 25-point Bernoulli problem the wrapper took 1.10 s before and 1.12 s after, with identical predictions. There was no measurable saving at this scale, and the change would affect every Laplace user, so we left it alone. It may be worth revisiting for larger n.

## Implementation Plan

1. Move the prototype to `GPy/inference/hyperparameters/inla.py` with a public class and docstrings.
2. Raise when free parameters lack priors.
3. Raise on an indefinite Hessian.
4. Measure the Laplace warm-start saving (no saving found; left unchanged).
5. Add tests: weights sum to one; model state restored; agreement with dense quadrature at d = 1 for both designs; collapse to MAP under a tight prior; error paths.
6. Add a short notebook comparing MAP, INLA-style and HMC (via the existing sampler) on a small classification problem.

## Backward Compatibility

None. The wrapper is additive and opt-in; no existing class or default changes. No existing code is changed.

## Testing Strategy

As in Implementation Plan step 5 (`GPy/testing/test_inla_hyperparameters.py`), plus an HMC comparison on one problem to check the hyperparameter posterior itself, not just the predictions.
